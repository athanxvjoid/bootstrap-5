Streaming got convenient. It didn't get better.

Compressed audio and a phone in your pocket solved a real problem: access. But convenience came with trade-offs most listeners never actually chose — lost dynamic range, a screen between you and the album, a queue that never really ends.

A turntable puts a boundary back around listening. One side, roughly twenty minutes, then you get up and choose again.

Full dynamic range

No loudness normalization, no lossy encoding between the mix and your ears.

One album, start to finish

Sequencing was part of the art. A record plays the way it was mixed.

Nothing to update

No firmware, no account, no app that changes its layout every few months.

Something to hold

Cover art at full size, liner notes, a physical object that marks the occasion.

Three parts, each doing one job well.

Plinth

Cast aluminum core, walnut or ash veneer, isolated on three damped feet.

Its only job is to disappear — no resonance passed up into the platter.

Platter

Machined aluminum, 1.6 kg, topped with a felt-free acrylic mat.

Mass and balance keep speed steady from the first groove to the last.

Tonearm

Carbon-fiber tube, gimbal bearings, dial-set anti-skate.

Light enough to track accurately, rigid enough to stay still.
